\Page{01 · Abstract and reader map}{Can language become a useful weight edit?}
Neural networks normally acquire their parameters through numerical optimization. Once trained, their weights determine how an input is transformed into a prediction. Weight-space programming asks whether a language model can inspect these stored parameters and write useful changes directly. We follow that question through two controlled targets.

\subsection*{Abstract}
UCI Adult is a census-income classification dataset: our small neural network predicts whether income exceeds \$50,000 from encoded census attributes. SmolLM2 is an intact 1.7-billion-parameter base language model, evaluated through the likelihood of multiple-choice answer continuations. Sol and Astra are two configured language-model editors. They search independently from a common origin; a cooperative proposal--review stage can use their development information to propose its own intervention.

On Adult, Sol's selected patch raises balanced accuracy, the equal mean of the two class recalls, by @sol_pp@ percentage points. It rescues 65 positive decisions and regresses 102 negative decisions, changing ordinary accuracy by @acc_pp@ percentage points (a decrease). Cooperation selects a related patch but does not establish an advantage over Sol. On the intact language model, genuine nonzero proposals are evaluated, yet the selection rule retains the original weights in every condition.

Exact interventions, explicit selection and paired decisions make the independent/cooperative comparison traceable and explain what a small score change means.

\Page{02 · Motivation and reference}{A research article opens the question}
\Image{reference-article-title.png}{The actual opening of the research blog article by Rikiya Takehi, Jayden Teoh, Alex Zhang and Omar Khattab, published 2 November 2026. This is a source-title excerpt, not a journal or arXiv cover; it motivates the experiment rather than supplying our measurements.}

The reference is \emph{Sparks of Weight-Space Programming: LLMs can natively read \& write neural network weights}. Weight-space programming treats stored parameters as objects that an editor can inspect, describe and change. Instead of asking a language model only for a prediction, the system asks it to write part of the numerical rule used by another network.

Takehi and colleagues demonstrate parameter reading and writing, task recognition, policy repair and small game networks, alongside a Qwen language-model example. These demonstrations make direct editing an empirical question. A meaningful test must still specify which parameters can change, what feedback an editor receives and how a proposed improvement is selected.

Our targets and restrictions differ from the reference. We examine an independent-versus-cooperative comparison on a damaged income classifier and an intact compact language model. We do not reproduce all of the article's headline results or import its benchmark scores into our result tables.

\Page{03 · Concepts before measurements}{Weights persist; activations respond}
A \emph{weight} is a stored numerical coefficient. The same coefficient participates in the computation for many different inputs. A \emph{bias} is a stored additive offset. An \emph{activation} is an intermediate value computed for a particular input. A \emph{tensor} is a numerical array; a matrix of weights is a two-dimensional tensor. These distinctions matter because a parameter edit changes a shared computation, while its effect on activations depends on the input.

During conventional training, an optimizer uses derivatives of a loss to update parameters. Our Adult network was trained that way. During the subsequent editing stage, the language-model editor proposes a schema-constrained intervention without gradient descent. The target performs forward computations, and the controller returns permitted development scores.

\Graphic{intervention}{The direct-editing loop. Every proposal is applied to the same immutable origin, validated after casting, and evaluated on development data. The loop can return development feedback; the final panel evaluates only the frozen selection. Arrows represent information flow, not differentiation.}

A \emph{candidate} is one complete proposed intervention relative to the origin. A \emph{patch} encodes its operations. A \emph{selected endpoint} is the candidate retained by the development rule. Candidate zero is the unchanged origin, represented by an empty patch. An editor can propose real changes and still select zero if no changed candidate wins.

Small changes can have several outcomes: confidence can move without a decision changing; a threshold can be crossed; or a downstream nonlinear gate can suppress the difference. A legal edit is therefore a computable proposal, not yet evidence of improvement.

\Page{04 · Research questions and design}{Four conditions, one starting point}
We ask three questions. Can a bounded direct edit improve the selected primary metric on frozen final data? What other decisions or probability scores change with it? Does development-informed cooperation outperform the independent editor endpoints?

Baseline means no editing. Sol denotes configured editor \texttt{@sol_model@}; Astra denotes \texttt{@astra_model@}. Both use @reasoning@ reasoning. Joint denotes the cooperative proposal--review condition using these two actors, rather than a third editor model. These are configured identities, not an inference from a model's self-description.

Joint can use both development histories but writes a full patch against that same origin. It does not apply Sol and then Astra in sequence; final answers supply no selection feedback.}

\subsection*{A fixed rule chooses the endpoint}
For Adult, selection maximizes development balanced accuracy; ties prefer a smaller executed delta norm, fewer changed scalars and then the earlier candidate index. The language-model task substitutes character-normalized development accuracy as its primary metric. Candidate zero is always eligible.

The study is one exploratory seed101 comparison. Original Adult solo allowances were ten candidates each, versus five shared joint rounds; LM v2 allowed five each. These unequal Adult allowances limit causal attribution to cooperation alone. Frozen assessment separates within-session selection from final comparison, while repeated-seed and repeated-session questions remain open.

\Page{05 · Dataset and partitioning}{What the Adult classifier predicts}
The UCI Adult, or Census Income, dataset derives from a 1994 census extraction. Its 48,842 original records contain 14 original input fields and a binary income target. Positive means income above \$50,000; negative means income at or below it. The name Adult identifies the dataset, not a neural-network architecture.

Original fields are age, workclass, final census weight (\texttt{fnlwgt}), education, education number, marital status, occupation, relationship, race, sex, capital gain, capital loss, hours per week and native country. This experiment excludes sex and race from encoded predictors. Their exclusion does not establish demographic independence of the remaining attributes.

\subsection*{Partition first; fit the encoder second}
The original 32,561 training records are stratified into fit (22,792), calibration (3,256) and development (6,513), using seed101. The original test split remains 16,281 rows: 3,846 positive and 12,435 negative. Fit data learn the encoder and network; calibration chooses the lesion; development guides editing; original test data assess frozen endpoints.

Within the full development partition, a fixed \textbf{2,048-row feedback panel} is actually scored per candidate. A separate fixed \textbf{256-row diagnostic panel} supports editing inspection. The 6,513 development total must not be mistaken for the number evaluated at each editing step.

Six numerical fields are standardized using fit-only means and standard deviations. Remaining categorical predictors become one-hot indicators, including a missing category; unseen categories map to all-zero indicators. The combined input has 104 dimensions. An input is a numerical representation of attributes, not a string prompt. No personal feature rows are included in this report.

\Page{06 · Network and mathematical definitions}{A complete small forward computation}
\Graphic{adultnet}{The Adult network maps 104 encoded inputs through hidden layers of 32 and 16 units to one positive-income probability. The diagram shows exact widths and nonlinearities, with schematic connections. All three linear layers have biases; only eligible second-layer weights are editable.}

For an encoded vector $x\in\mathbb R^{104}$, define
\begin{align*}
z^{(1)}_i&=\sum_{j=0}^{103}W^{(1)}_{ij}x_j+b^{(1)}_i,
&h^{(1)}_i&=\max(0,z^{(1)}_i),\\
z^{(2)}_r&=\sum_{i=0}^{31}W^{(2)}_{ri}h^{(1)}_i+b^{(2)}_r,
&h^{(2)}_r&=\max(0,z^{(2)}_r),\\
\ell&=\sum_{r=0}^{15}W^{(3)}_{0r}h^{(2)}_r+b^{(3)}_0,
&p&=\frac{1}{1+e^{-\ell}}.
\end{align*}
Here $j$ indexes inputs, $i$ indexes the 32 first hidden units and $r$ indexes the 16 second hidden units. $W^{(1)},W^{(2)},W^{(3)}$ are weight matrices with shapes $32\times104$, $16\times32$, $1\times16$. Each $b$ is the matching bias vector. Each $z$ is a preactivation and each $h$ is an activation after applying the rectified linear unit, or ReLU: retain positive values and replace negative values by zero.

The scalar $\ell$ is the logit. Sigmoid converts it to a probability $p$ between zero and one. The predicted label is positive when $p\ge0.5$. This threshold is held fixed for the paired decision comparisons.

Training uses CPU float32, 30 epochs, batches of 256 and Adam with learning rate 0.001 and binary cross entropy with logits. The later editing stage leaves training finished; it proposes bounded changes and evaluates forward passes. Float32 means coefficients and inference arithmetic use 32-bit floating-point values.

\Page{07 · Lesion and intervention surface}{A permanent mask sets the boundary}
\Graphic{mask}{Actual second-layer matrix in zero-based coordinates: 16 rows by 32 columns. Gray cells are 256 permanently zero weights; teal cells are the 256 surviving eligible weights. Amber outlines identify the 16 coordinates changed by each selected main edit. This is a weight mask, not an activation map.}

The calibration procedure tries zeroing 10\%, 25\% and 50\% of second-layer columns in a fixed random order, seeking a balanced-accuracy drop of at least 0.05. It never meets that damage criterion. The retained 50\% lesion records \texttt{insufficient\_damage=true}; its calibration balanced accuracy is approximately 0.794938. The damaged model nevertheless defines the common origin for this editing comparison.

Only surviving \texttt{fc2.weight} coefficients can change. Masked weights stay zero; biases and other layers stay fixed. Adult permits at most 10\% of all network parameters to differ and a delta norm no larger than 5\% of the editable origin tensor's norm. With 3,905 total parameters, the count ceiling is 390; the surviving surface itself has 256 scalars.

For origin $W^0$ and candidate $W'$, $\Delta W=W'-W^0$. The Frobenius norm $\|\Delta W\|_F=\sqrt{\sum_{r,c}(\Delta W_{rc})^2}$ combines scalar changes into one distance. Relative norm divides by $\|W^0\|_F$. Validation counts actual unequal values after float32 casting and measures the executed subtraction with double-precision norm aggregation. These are distances in parameter space, not between people.

\Page{08 · Editor interface and repeatable method}{Inspect, propose, evaluate, freeze}
An editor sees permitted numerical observations, the action schema and its own development feedback. It can inspect bounded information and request an evaluation within its allowance. The controller checks tensor names, indices, finite values, mask compliance, executed changed count and norm. Every evaluation reconstructs the candidate from the immutable origin.

\subsection*{Structured paraphrase of the prompt contract}
The following explains the interface; it is not a quotation or an inferred reasoning trace. Inspect permitted target information. Return a structured JSON action using sparse additions or selected-column scaling. Request only permitted development evaluation. Use feedback to propose a later candidate. Freeze selection before final assessment. Adult diagnostics and feedback are distinct; language-model inspection includes bounded tensor slices and preset training probes, without validation example text or final rows.

Exact resource texts are public in \texttt{prompts/v1} and \texttt{prompts/v2}; controller and schema sources define the accepted actions. Sparse additions identify row, column and delta. Column scaling identifies columns and a factor. A controller can reject an attempt without producing a score: a missing score is never evidence of poor measured accuracy.

\subsection*{Cooperation is an information treatment}
Independent Sol and Astra do not see each other's attempts. Joint receives their development histories, then runs five fresh proposal--review rounds. The reviewer may accept the full patch, revise it or reject it to no-op. Only the reviewed choice is evaluated; unmeasured drafts are not candidates with invented scores.

The final feedback cannot choose a patch, stopping point or winning editor. Selection uses the ordered rule already stated. To repeat the analysis, retain that rule as well as the exact prompt, origin, dtype, panels and schemas. Changing any of those would define a different experiment.

\Page{10 · From a scalar to a prediction}{What does one changed connection do?}
Consider coordinate $(0,15)$: the connection from first-layer activation 15 into second-layer unit zero. Its actual saved Sol values are
\[
\begin{aligned}
W^0_{0,15}&=@before@,\\
W^{\rm Sol}_{0,15}&=@after@.
\end{aligned}
\]
For a \emph{synthetic arithmetic illustration}, let the incoming activation $h^{(1)}_{15}=2$. This is an illustrative value, not a reconstructed held-out activation. The connection contributes approximately $0.249643460$ before the edit and $0.305643469$ after it: a preactivation difference of approximately $0.056000009$.

Holding first-layer activations fixed is legitimate because the edit changes only the second layer. The full preactivation difference is
\[
\Delta z^{(2)}_0=\sum_{c\in C}(W^{\rm Sol}_{0c}-W^0_{0c})h^{(1)}_c,
\]
where $C$ is the set of 16 selected columns. ReLU then gives $h'^{(2)}_0=\max(0,z^{(2)}_0+\Delta z^{(2)}_0)$. The output logit depends on that activation and the fixed final-layer coefficient, before sigmoid yields $p'$.

Changed scalars are counts; L2 is absolute parameter-change norm and relative L2 divides by the origin second-layer norm. These describe intervention size, not performance; all three patches satisfy the same legal bounds.}

The synthetic multiplication explains how a real weight participates in computation. Saved final probabilities, rather than this illustration, establish what happened to actual anonymous test decisions.

\Page{12 · Metrics before the main result}{Two meanings of a correct classifier}
For a true positive class of size $N_+=3846$ and a true negative class of size $N_-=12435$, let $TP$ count correctly predicted positives and $TN$ correctly predicted negatives. Positive recall is $TP/N_+$; negative recall is $TN/N_-$. Then
\[
\mathrm{BA}=\frac12\left(\frac{TP}{N_+}+\frac{TN}{N_-}\right),\qquad
\mathrm{Accuracy}=\frac{TP+TN}{N_++N_-}.
\]
Balanced accuracy, abbreviated BA, gives each class half the total weight. Ordinary accuracy gives each example equal weight, so the more numerous negatives carry more total weight. Both are fractions between zero and one; multiplying by 100 expresses a percentage. A difference of 0.01 is one percentage point, abbreviated pp.

\Graphic{mainresult}{Selected Adult final scores on the same 16,281 original test rows, shown as percentages. Higher is better on both axes. Explicit linear axes begin at 78\%, so dots encode position rather than zero-based bar area. Sol and joint improve class-balanced performance while losing ordinary accuracy.}

These are two summaries of the same decisions, not competing estimates of one unnamed quality. If an edit correctly identifies more positive cases but wrongly labels additional negatives, BA can rise while ordinary accuracy falls. Which objective is appropriate depends on how a task values the errors; this experiment declares BA primary and retains the other scores.

The next table gives full displayed percentages and total correct counts. Definitions come first so the difference cannot be mistaken for inconsistent measurement.

\subsection*{What is actually compared?}
Baseline is the damaged common origin, not the unlesioned clean model. Each edited endpoint is a complete intervention from that origin, selected by development before final measurement. The displayed ordering is an outcome of this seed and session, rather than a ranking of general editor ability.

The point estimates answer whether a selected patch changed performance on this saved panel. To understand why the scores moved, we next examine paired correctness transitions. To assess whether the result amounts to repair or cooperative superiority, we then bring in probability quality, controls and direct paired uncertainty.

\Page{14 · Paired decisions and exact algebra}{65 rescues and 102 regressions}
A rescue changes a wrong decision into a correct one; a regression changes a correct decision into a wrong one. These are paired counts because both predictions refer to the same example. Sol rescues 65 positive examples and regresses 102 negative examples, with no transitions in the opposite class-specific directions.

\Graphic{counts}{Paired correctness changes against damaged baseline on the 16,281-row test panel. Teal shows positive rescues; rose shows negative regressions. Lengths encode raw example counts on a common scale, not percentage points. Joint has 59 positive rescues and 96 negative regressions; Astra has 4 and 13.}

\[
\begin{aligned}
\Delta\mathrm{BA}_{\rm Sol}&=\frac12\left(\frac{65}{3846}-\frac{102}{12435}\right),\\
\Delta\mathrm{Accuracy}_{\rm Sol}&=\frac{65-102}{16281}=\frac{-37}{16281}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The first expression yields @sol_pp@ pp; the second yields @acc_pp@ pp. Sixty-five rescues matter more per case to BA because positives are less numerous. Ordinary accuracy instead registers the net loss of 37 correct decisions, from 13,490 to 13,453.

\Page{15 · Controls and probability quality}{A balanced gain is not restoration}
ROC-AUC measures the probability ranking of positives above negatives, allowing for ties. Log-loss averages the negative log probability assigned to the true class; confident wrong predictions incur a large penalty. Brier score averages squared probability error. Higher is better for BA, accuracy and ROC-AUC; lower is better for log-loss and Brier.

\Data{quality}{All seven frozen Adult states on the same 16,281 original test examples. Entries are unitless fractions rounded to six decimals; BA/accuracy/ROC-AUC are higher-better and log-loss/Brier lower-better. Clean improves ordinary accuracy and probability quality over damaged baseline but has lower BA. Sol and joint shift class errors while worsening probability losses.}

The clean state is the pre-lesion network. It outperforms damaged baseline in ordinary accuracy, AUC and both probability losses, yet has lower BA. Together with the unmet calibration damage criterion, this makes a BA recovery percentage inappropriate: clean-minus-damaged BA would be a negative denominator. We compare endpoints rather than describing a restored clean decision rule.

Random and grid are frozen development-selected controls, not favorable perturbations chosen after seeing the test. Random selects a 64-scalar patch; grid selects surviving-column scaling by 1.02. Their retained results help contextualize changes available on this small parameter surface. They do not isolate all effects of search effort or establish superiority of language-model reasoning across sessions.

The probability scores add another qualification to the Adult result: a better balance of thresholded class decisions does not imply better probability estimates. A result table with only BA would miss this tradeoff.

\Page{17 · Second target and task definitions}{An intact compact language model}
SmolLM2-1.7B base is a compact decoder language model with approximately 1.7 billion parameters. It assigns probabilities to token continuations. Unlike the Adult target, it is intact: no artificial lesion is applied and no damaged-model recovery claim is appropriate.

ARC-Easy is the easy partition of the AI2 Reasoning Challenge, a multiple-choice grade-school science benchmark. PIQA, Physical Interaction Question Answering, tests physical commonsense with answer choices. In this protocol ARC supplies development information; PIQA is final-only. Their outcomes remain separate because they test different tasks.

\Page{18 · Transformer mathematics}{Inside one gated MLP}
\Graphic{gated}{One gated multilayer perceptron inside a transformer block: residual width 2,048 expands to 8,192 features and returns to 2,048. Separate gate and up projections meet through coordinatewise multiplication; a residual bypass adds the input back. Geometry is schematic; no fabricated coefficient or activation values are shown.}

For incoming residual $r\in\mathbb R^{2048}$, let $x=\mathrm{RMSNorm}(r)$. RMSNorm means root mean square normalization, with learned rescaling. Define
\[
g=W_gx,\quad u=W_ux,\quad h=\mathrm{SiLU}(g)\odot u,
\qquad r'=r+W_dh.
\]
$W_g$ and $W_u$ map 2,048 coordinates to 8,192; $g$ is the gate vector and $u$ the up-projected vector. SiLU is the sigmoid linear unit, $\mathrm{SiLU}(t)=t/(1+e^{-t})$, applied coordinatewise. The symbol $\odot$ multiplies corresponding coordinates. $W_d$ maps the gated features $h$ back to 2,048 coordinates, and $r'$ is the residual after adding this MLP contribution.

Only down-projection weights $W_d$ are eligible for editing. A column controls how one expanded feature contributes across output coordinates. Changing that column changes the shared map, while its actual effect still depends on the input's gated features. The real intervention on the next page uses columns, not invented individual LM scalar samples.

\Page{19 · Real language-model interventions}{A legal proposal can remain unselected}
LM proposals may scale selected down-projection columns or add sparse scalar deltas. Actual changed scalars cannot exceed 0.1\% of unique model parameters; each changed tensor must remain within 5\% relative Frobenius change. Counts and norms describe executed, post-cast changes from the common intact origin.

\Graphic{columns}{Actual Astra candidate 1 targets the last block's down projection: all 2,048 rows in columns 0--31 are multiplied by 0.5. Bundles are separated and widths are explicitly not proportional. The operation changes 65,536 weights; the candidate is nonselected, so this image does not describe the final endpoint.}

The exact tensor is \texttt{model.layers.23.mlp.down\_proj.weight}; block 23 is the last of 24 zero-indexed blocks. Thirty-two columns times 2,048 rows gives 65,536 actual changes. Its measured delta norm is @lm_norm@ and relative norm is @lm_relative@. This is a genuine nonzero intervention satisfying the allowed surface and size constraints.

Raw before-and-after scalar samples for this LM candidate were not retained in approved evidence. The patch operation, measured count, norms and executed tensor hashes establish its geometry without inventing plausible-looking coefficients.

An intervention can be legal and nonzero yet fail to improve the primary development score. It can also tie the unchanged model. Since the selection rule prefers smaller norm at equal score, a nonzero tie loses to candidate zero. Selection and proposal generation are distinct empirical events.

\Page{20 · Answer likelihood and normalization}{What makes one answer the prediction?}
Let $q$ denote question context and $a_{j,t}$ token $t$ of answer continuation $j$. If continuation $j$ has $T_j$ tokens, its likelihood score is
\[
L_j=\sum_{t=1}^{T_j}\log P_\theta(a_{j,t}\mid q,a_{j,<t}).
\]
$P_\theta$ is the target language model with parameters $\theta$; $a_{j,<t}$ means earlier tokens in that answer. The score adds token log probabilities. Larger, or less negative, means the whole continuation has greater conditional likelihood under this scoring convention.

\[
\hat j_{\rm raw}=\arg\max_jL_j,\qquad
\hat j_{\rm norm}=\arg\max_j\frac{L_j}{C_j}.
\]
Argmax means choose the index with the greatest score. $C_j$ is the Python character length of the choice text: \textbf{not token count}. A tie takes the first maximum in native choice order. Raw accuracy, \texttt{acc}, and character-normalized accuracy, \texttt{acc\_norm}, each average whether its chosen index equals the gold label.

\subsection*{Synthetic scoring illustration}
Suppose invented choices A and B have log-likelihood sums $-4$ and $-6$, and character lengths 4 and 12. Raw choice is A because $-4>-6$. Normalized scores are $-1$ and $-0.5$, so normalized choice is B. These numbers explain the scoring rule; they are not dataset results or copied question text.

The native harness constructs contexts and continuations using the pinned task definitions. The public numeric records retain likelihoods, character/token lengths and gold choices so both conventions can be recomputed offline. Token lengths remain audit information, without becoming the normalization divisor.

\Page{23 · Discussion and reproduction}{What the evidence adds, and how to inspect it}
The first question receives different answers on the two targets. Adult's bounded edit improves the declared primary metric while reducing total correct decisions and worsening probability losses. The paired algebra explains the change as an error tradeoff. On intact SmolLM2, legal proposals are explored but none displaces the unchanged origin under development selection. A useful editing study must retain both kinds of outcome.

The second question concerns cooperation. Joint has no demonstrated advantage over Sol here: its Adult point estimate is lower with a direct interval crossing zero, while the LM endpoints are identical. A common origin and explicit information flow make the comparison interpretable, but one seed/session and unequal Adult opportunities cannot establish a general cooperation effect.
